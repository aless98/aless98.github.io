Percutaneous epicardial access requires advancing a needle toward the pericardial space with very little visual feedback, making the procedure heavily dependent on the operator's tactile judgement. This work maps the physics of needle-tissue interaction onto a synthesised auditory signal, so that changes in tissue resistance and proximity become audible and complement the clinician's haptic and visual cues.
